# MarketPulse UK

**Register:** product

## Users

Commercial finance leads and brand managers at Damm UK who run the monthly close meeting. They open this dashboard on a laptop or projected screen in a room with their director, and inside one minute need to be able to say: how we closed, why we're up or down, which promotions worked, and what we plan for next month.

## Product purpose

A one-screen close cockpit. No tabs, no drill-downs, no navigation. Everything an operator needs to lead the close meeting is in one viewport, with an AI analyst sidecar that narrates the data and answers follow-ups.

## Tone

Operator voice, not engineer voice. "Where demand came from", not "Channel decomposition". "Did they work?", not "Lift analysis". Numbers are formatted for humans (April 2026, not 2026-04). Honest about uncertainty: confidence is content, not chrome.

## Strategic principles

- **The story leads. Numbers support.** The headline of the dashboard is the answer to "how did we close?", written as a sentence with the numbers inline. Not a giant percentage.
- **One screen, one register.** No tabs, no modals, no drill-downs. The AI analyst is the escape hatch.
- **Confidence is visible.** Sample counts, uncertainty bands, "based on N past promotions" labels render as first-class content.
- **The AI panel narrates the cockpit.** It is not a chatbot stapled to a dashboard; it is the voice of the dashboard.
- **Workflow continuation.** The dashboard should lead naturally into "share the close summary with my team."

## Anti-references

- Default SaaS BI tools (Tableau, Power BI) with their identical card grids, generic chart legends, ROI gauges, and engineer-speak headers.
- The "hero metric template": giant number, small label, supporting tiles, gradient accent. The numbers are real, but they belong in a sentence, not on a billboard.

## Color strategy

Restrained. Tinted neutrals with a faint warm bias (a quiet nod to Damm's brand red without committing the surface to it). Accent usage ≤ 10%: sky blue for "the future" (projections), emerald/red for variance signs, amber only for low-confidence warnings.

## Theme

Light by default. Scene: a brand manager and a finance lead in a meeting room at 10am, walking through last month's numbers on a laptop with the projector cable plugged in. Dark mode supported because some users keep their OS in dark; light is the canonical render.
